SLS 3D printers use thermoplastic polymer powder as raw material, producing high-quality, highly durable parts with no support structures

SLA 3D printers use liquid photopolymer resin, printing high-resolution parts with a smooth surface finish from a wide range of thermoset materials

FDM 3D printers extrude thermoplastic filament, enabling lightweight models thanks to partial infill and low-cost parts from a wide range of thermoplastics

MJF 3D printers use thermoplastic raw material in powder form. Printing is based on print heads that precisely jet droplets of heat-absorbing (fusing) and heat-inhibiting (detailing) agents onto the powder bed, while infrared lamps heat the surface in the same pass, solidifying the powder that absorbed the fusing agent

CFR 3D printers build parts with a composite structure — a polymer matrix reinforced by a continuous fiber — improving strength and stiffness to the point of replacing lightweight metal parts

The thermoplastic "Nylon", professionally known as polyamide, was first invented by DuPont in 1935. It was initially used for toothbrushes and stockings, and today it is used for strong, durable engineering parts

"Nylon" bags are not actually made of nylon — they are usually made of LDPE polyethylene, one of the most common polymers in industry, alongside the most widespread industrial polymer, polypropylene (PP) — a winning combination of low cost, low specific weight and exceptional chemical resistance, ideal for food packaging, bottles, the automotive industry and medical equipment

Systematics was founded in 1979 and today employs over 180 skilled professionals across GIS, CAD & PLM systems, algorithmics and simulation tools, 3D printing technologies, and digital manufacturing & operations systems

Formlabs is one of the world's leading and most influential 3D printing companies. Founded in 2011 by three students from an MIT lab, it employs today around 800 people worldwide and offers professional industrial printing solutions, including the Form 4 SLA series and the Fuse SLS series — the best-selling SLS printer in the world

3D printing was invented in 1984 by engineer Chuck Hull, who developed the first SLA printer and patented the process. The biggest leap in the 3D printing market happened in the last decade — from prototyping to mass production of end-use parts. As of 2026 the global 3D printing market is estimated at $28-35 billion, and is expected to cross the $100 billion mark within the next decade

3D printers are very common in medicine. Every person's anatomy is unique, and 3D printing is the only manufacturing method in the world that enables complex, personally customized products (Mass Customization) quickly and at low cost. Dentistry, orthodontics, temporary and permanent crowns, dentures, anatomical models for medical simulations, orthopedic splints and life-saving surgical guides are just a few of the medical applications

Coatings and post-processing of printed parts are critical steps in turning a printed part from a raw prototype into a functional industrial end product. These processes include painting, surface smoothing by mechanical polishing or chemical processes, metal or ceramic coatings that provide UV and chemical resistance for improved environmental durability, and embedding inserts such as threaded inserts, locating pins, wear pins and magnets that upgrade parts to a higher-grade product

The manufacturing process strongly affects product properties because it shapes the material's microstructure. Take the same raw material, such as ABS, and compare its properties when 3D printed, injection molded or CNC machined — you will find very different properties. Although the chemical composition stays the same, the way the molecules align, cool and bond changes completely between manufacturing methods
